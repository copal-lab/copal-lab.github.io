Our research

Relational Learning & Reasoning

How do animals and humans learn relations between stimuli, and how do they use what they have learned to make judgments in new situations? We investigate these questions through nonverbal tasks that allow us to compare cognition across species.

01 / Learning

Learning Nonverbal Relations

How do organisms learn relationships among stimuli, and when do they respond to those relationships rather than to the absolute properties of individual stimuli?

Research topic

Transposition

Learning and generalizing relations such as larger than or brighter than across different sets of stimuli.

02 / Using learned relations

Manipulating Nonverbal Relations

Can organisms use previously learned relations to respond appropriately to new combinations of stimuli? Different tasks let us examine what is learned and how that knowledge is used.

Research topic

Transitive Inference

What can choices between previously unpaired items tell us about how animals represent ordered relations?

Conceptual question

Transitivity

How is transitive responding related to the logical property of transitivity, and what can behavioral tests establish?

Research topic

Transitive Inference with Lists

Investigating how separately learned sequences can support judgments about relations between items from different lists.

03 / Developing research

Conditional Reasoning

We are developing nonverbal approaches to studying conditional reasoning. More about this work will appear here as the project develops.
